# Rev 2 architecture: Model I → ECB backplane + daughter cards (proposal)

Rev 0.1 was a single board. Rev 2 splits the system:

```
 Model I bus ──┐                       ECB bus (DIN 41612 C96, 5 slots)
 (J1 edge)     │   BACKPLANE           ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
               ├─► bridge logic ──────►│ Slot 1   │ Slot 2   │ Slots 3-5│
 EI ribbon ◄───┘   5 V power in        │ FPGA card│ SD card  │ spare    │
 (J2 header)       power switch, LED   │ Tang 20K │ FreHD    │          │
                                       └──────────┴──────────┴──────────┘
```

Everything stays **through-hole**: the DIN 41612 sockets on the backplane, right-angle DIN 41612 plugs on the cards, DIP logic, and modules on pin headers.

## Backplane: Model I → ECB bridge

The Model I bus gives memory strobes (RD*, WR*), I/O strobes (IN*, OUT*), INTAK*, SYSRES*, WAIT* and INT*, but no MREQ*, IORQ*, M1*, RFSH* or clock. A GAL rebuilds the Z80-style signals:

| ECB signal | Made from | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A0–A15 | Model I A0–A15 | Two 74HCT244, always driving |
| D0–D7 | Model I D0–D7 | **Unbuffered pass-through.** Each card buffers its own data, as rev 0.1 already does. A backplane buffer would need to know when *any* card is being read, which ECB has no signal for |
| MREQ* | RD* or WR* low | GAL |
| IORQ* | IN* or OUT* or INTAK* low | GAL |
| RD* | RD* or IN* low | GAL |
| WR* | WR* or OUT* low | GAL |
| M1* | INTAK* low | GAL. So IORQ* + M1* = interrupt acknowledge, as on a Z80 |
| RESET* | SYSRES* | |
| EI RD*/WR* (J2 pins 16/14) | Model I RD*/WR*, chosen by JP8/JP9 | **1-2 (direct):** straight from the Model I, so the EI works even with the backplane switched off, and with no added delay. **2-3 (via GAL, rev 2.1):** withheld for 8000h–FFFFh while ECB c10 (EXTMEM*) is low, so the EI's own RAM stays off the bus. Needed with the banked-memory card, and the backplane must then be on whenever the Model I is |
| WAIT*, INT* | cards → Model I | Open collector, with pull-ups on the backplane (ECB side) and optional 4.7k pull-ups R3/R4 on the Model I side, driven onto the Model I bus through a 74HC07 (CMOS inputs: no input current, low up to 1.35 V, which suits the FPGA card's diode BUSY→WAIT path) |
| RFSH*, HALT*, BUSAK*, NMI*, BUSRQ* | — | Pulled to inactive. The Model I bus can't supply them |
| CLK | — | Not available from the Model I. Cards must not depend on it |

The backplane also has:
- **J1:** the Model I card-edge fingers, as on rev 0.1.
- **J2:** the 2 × 20 IDC header that passes the bus through to the Expansion Interface.
- **Power:** 5 V input (terminal block plus barrel jack), a power switch and an LED, as in the reference.

### Backplane as built (`hardware/backplane`)

Generated by `hardware/tools/gen_backplane.py` (schematic) and `gen_backplane_pcb.py` (board bootstrap, via the shared `pcbgen.py`). Once you edit either file in KiCad, edit it there; don't re-run the generators.

- **Board:** 196.6 × 108 mm, **4 layers**, with a 56 × 10 mm card-edge tab for J1 on the bottom edge. GND pours fill the spare copper on all four layers.
  - Two layers were enough for 3 slots but not for 5. Every ECB line is bused, used or not, and between DIN pin rows there is room for only one track per layer. The 2-layer attempt stalled at about 30 unrouted connections.
- **Left side:**
  - Top: power entry. J3 barrel jack and J4 terminal block feed F1 (MF-RHT300 polyfuse), then JP6 (power-switch header) onto +5V. JP7 links the Model I edge +5V to the backplane +5V; leave it open unless you mean to share supplies.
  - Middle: U1–U4 (2 × 74HCT244, the ECB_BRIDGE GAL, 74HC07), the RN1/RN2 resistor packs, R2 (EXTMEM* pull-up), and R3/R4, the optional Model I-side WAIT*/INT* pull-ups.
  - JP8/JP9 (above J2) choose the EI's RD*/WR* source. Fit both shunts on 1-2 (direct) unless the banked-memory card is fitted, then both on 2-3 (via GAL). With the card enabled and the shunts on 1-2, the EI's RAM and the card would both drive 8000h–FFFFh.
  - **Power:** the Model I drives this board's 244s and GAL directly. Switch the backplane on together with, or before, the Model I; an unpowered backplane is partly powered through those inputs' protection diodes. If you do run the Model I with the backplane off (JP8/JP9 on 1-2), leave R3/R4 unfitted, because they would pull WAIT*/INT* toward the unpowered rail.
  - Bottom: J2 IDC to the EI, directly above the J1 fingers.
- **Right side:** J11–J15 DIN 41612 C96 sockets (5 slots) at 20.32 mm pitch. To the right of each socket: a 100 nF capacitor by pin 1 (+5V), the chain-bridge header JPn level with BAI/BAO and IEI/IEO, and a 10 µF capacitor by pin 32 (GND).
- **Chain jumpers:** on JP1–JP5 (one per slot), 1–2 bridges BAI→BAO and 3–4 bridges IEI→IEO. Fit both on any slot that is empty, or holds a card that doesn't pass the chains through.
- **Checks:** schematic ERC is clean, and all 96 slot pins match the reference ECB netlist.
- **Routing:** Freerouting 2.5.0 on 4 layers, 0.3 mm signals and 1.0 mm power, about 2 minutes. Always run it with `--router.optimizer.enabled=false`: on the 3-slot board its optimizer took 49 minutes per pass and improved nothing. `pcb_fixup.py` gave six GND pads solid zone connections to fix starved thermals.
- **Results:** DRC with schematic parity gives 0 violations, 0 unconnected pads and 0 footprint errors. Views: `trs80_backplane_routed.svg` (all four copper layers) and `trs80_backplane_3d.png`. Order it as a 4-layer, 1.6 mm board.
- **To check before ordering:** whether the J4 terminal block's wire entry faces a sensible direction, the 3D clearance of the DIN sockets against your card guides, and the long-standing rev 0.1 item about the J1 finger geometry.

## Daughter card 1: graphics + Am9511 (Tang Nano 20K)

This is rev 0.1's logic, minus the Model I connectors, on an ECB Eurocard:
- **Unchanged:** the IDT7132, both 74HCT245s, the 2N7000 level shifters, the CD74HC4050, and WAIT/INT outputs onto ECB WAIT*/INT*.
- **Decode GALs** rewritten for ECB signals: memory = MREQ* & (RD* | WR*), I/O = IORQ* & (RD* | WR*) & M1* high. Because the card now follows ECB rules, it could in principle also work in a real Z80 ECB system.
- **Tang Nano 20K** in place of the 9K: 2 × 20 headers, rows 0.8" apart (measure a real module to confirm). The 2.4× logic removes today's 80% squeeze.
- **Pin budget:** of the 20K's 34 header I/Os, 30 are cleanly usable. The others drive the on-board RGB LED or the audio amplifier enable, or connect to the BL616 USB chip. The card needs 30, or 29 if the IDT7132 BUSY_R input is replaced by write-and-verify in the FPGA, which leaves one spare.
- **Free on the 20K:** HDMI has its own pins, so a later "card drives its own display" feature needs no header pins.

### FPGA card as built (`hardware/card-fpga`)

Generated by `hardware/tools/gen_card_fpga.py` (schematic) and `gen_card_fpga_pcb.py` (board, via `pcbgen.py`). Both use the shared ECB pinout in `hardware/tools/ecb.py`.

- **Circuit:** rev 0.1's circuit unchanged apart from these intended differences, confirmed by a netlist diff against rev 0.1:
  - J1 is a DIN 41612 C96 male right-angle plug. It replaces the Model I edge, the EI header and the barrel jack (+5V now comes from ECB), and the J4 spare-I/O header is gone.
  - **U4 `hardware/gal/ecb_mem_decode.pld`:** DP_CE_N = MREQ* & (RD* | WR*) in 0x3000–0x37DF, or to 0x37FF with FULL. MREQ* replaces rev 0.1's MEMQ, and the RD*/WR* term skips Z80 refresh cycles.
  - **U5 `hardware/gal/ecb_io_decode.pld`:** IORD/IOWR = IORQ* & M1* high & RD*/WR* on 0x50–0x53 (0x70–0x73 with ALT). M1* high keeps interrupt acknowledge out.
  - **U3** takes its direction from ECB RD* instead of IN*. On ECB, IN = IORQ* + RD*.
  - **U6** buffers ECB RESET* in place of SYSRES*.
  - BAI→BAO and IEI→IEO are linked on the card, so the chains pass through.
- **Tang Nano 20K pin use.** Header positions are counted from the USB-C end.

| Left header | Net | Right header | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 P73 | RA0 | 1 5V | TANG_5V (via D2) |
| 2 P74 | RA1 | 2 GND | GND |
| 3 P75 (HSPI) | — | 3 P76 (HSPI) | — |
| 4 P85 | RA2 | 4 P80 | FD0 |
| 5 P77 | RA3 | 5 P42 | FD1 |
| 6 P15 (LED0) | R_CE_N | 6 P41 | FD2 |
| 7 P16 (LED1) | R_RW_N | 7 P56 | FD3 |
| 8 P27 | RA4 | 8 P54 | FD4 |
| 9 P28 | RA5 | 9 P51 (PA_EN) | — (driven low) |
| 10 P25 | RA6 | 10 P48 | FD5 |
| 11 P26 | RA7 | 11 P55 | FD6 |
| 12 P29 | RA8 | 12 P49 | FD7 |
| 13 P30 | RA9 | 13 P86 | WAIT_DRV |
| 14 P31 | RA10 | 14 P79 (WS2812) | — (driven low) |
| 15 P17 (LED2) | PORT_OE_N | 15 GND | GND |
| 16 P20 (LED5) | RESET_3V3_N | 16 3V3 | +3V3 |
| 17 P19 (LED4) | R_BUSY_3V3_N | 17 P72 | INT_DRV |
| 18 P18 (LED3) | IORD_3V3 | 18 P71 | IOWR_3V3 |
| 19 3V3 | +3V3 | 19 P53 | PA0_3V3 |
| 20 GND | GND | 20 P52 | PA1_3V3 |

  - All 30 usable header I/Os are used, so none are spare. BUSY_R is kept.
  - The module LED pins carry only active-low outputs (which have 10k pull-ups) or push-pull inputs from U6. The LEDs therefore show activity: LED0 DPRAM access, LED1 FPGA write, LED2 port cycle, LED3 lit except during IN, LED4 collision, LED5 reset.
  - The open-drain gate drivers (WAIT_DRV, INT_DRV) avoid the pins known to be pulled up on the module (the DDC, TF-slot and LED pins), so the 10k pull-downs can hold them off while the FPGA configures. See check 3 below.
  - Keep the module's TF slot empty: P80 and P85 are its data lines.
- **FPGA build:** `fpga/build20k.tcl` builds for GW2AR-LV18QN88C8/I7, with top `src/trs80_card20k_top.v` (a wrapper around the unchanged core) and constraints in `constraints/trs80_card20k.cst`.
  - **Result:** 33% logic (6,809 of 20,736), 8 of 46 BSRAM, Fmax 58.3 MHz against 27 MHz.
  - **Expected warning PR1014:** the 27 MHz clock pin (P4) is a PLL input, not a GCLK pin. Timing still closes with plenty of margin.
  - **Simulation:** `fpga/sim/run_sim.ps1 -Tang20K` runs the top-level testbench through the 20K wrapper, and it passes, as do the 9K top, sprite and APU suites. OSS CAD Suite's `bin` and `lib` must be on PATH.
- **Board:** 160 × 100 mm Eurocard. J1 is on the rear (left) edge, and the Tang Nano's USB-C and the ACT/PWR LEDs face the front edge. 2.5 mm strips along the top and bottom edges are kept clear for the card guides.
  - **Routing:** Freerouting with `--router.optimizer.enabled=false`, 15 passes in about 2 minutes, 0 unrouted.
  - **Clean-up:** `hardware/tools/pcb_fixup.py` finished the board by giving starved GND thermals solid connections, fixing escaped net names and moving clashing reference text. It hid the references of R1, R2, C4 and Q10.
  - **Results:** DRC with schematic parity gives 0 violations, 0 unconnected pads and 0 footprint errors.
  - **Views:** `trs80_card_fpga_routed.svg` and `trs80_card_fpga_3d.png`.

**Check before ordering the FPGA card:**
1. Tang Nano 20K header row spacing. The footprint assumes 20.32 mm (0.8"); measure a module.
2. Tang Nano 20K orientation. Pads 1 and 21 are at the USB-C end, and the left header carries P73…GND. Check against the Sipeed pinout with the module component-side up.
3. Whether P86 and P72 have anything on the module that pulls them high, which would assert WAIT/INT while the FPGA configures.
4. The IDT7132 pinout (carried over from rev 0.1).

## Daughter card 2: SD-card storage (FreHD-compatible)

The target is the FreHD register set on ports C0h–CFh, which emulates the Radio Shack hard-disk controller with `.HDV` images on a FAT SD card. The existing FreHD drivers and utilities for LDOS, LS-DOS, NEWDOS and CP/M would then work unchanged. The card needs:
- **Decode:** a GAL for ports C0h–CFh, a 74HCT245 for data, and an open-collector WAIT output.
- **Controller:** see the open decision below.

### SD card as built (`hardware/card-sd`, `firmware/sdcard`)

Generated by `hardware/tools/gen_card_sd.py` and `gen_card_sd_pcb.py`.

- **Bus interface:**
  - U2, an ATF16V8B (`hardware/gal/ecb_sd_decode.pld`), decodes C0h–CFh as IORQ* with M1* high and EN (JP1).
  - It gives the Pico active-high SELRD/SELWR and enables U1 (74HCT245) for the data.
  - It pulls ECB WAIT* low, as an emulated open drain, until the Pico raises ACK. The Pico can therefore take as long as it needs, from core 1 for register access and from core 0 for disk work, which shows as BUSY.
  - ACK has a pull-up. With no Pico running, the card never holds WAIT and reads return FFh.
- **Level shifting:**
  - Data: U1 → XD → 2N7000 bidirectional shifters → PD0–7 on Pico GP0–7.
  - A0–A3, SELRD, SELWR and RESET* go through CD74HC4050 buffers.
  - ACK is 3.3 V into the GAL's TTL-level input.
- **Other parts:**
  - Raspberry Pi Pico socketed on two 1×20 headers, with VSYS fed from +5V through a 1N5819.
  - SD card on a breakout module in J2, a 1×8 socket wired in Adafruit #254 order (5V 3V GND CLK DO DI CS CD), on SPI0. The module is fitted with a straight header pointing down, so it lies flat over the marked area beside J2 at about 12 mm high, which clears the next card at 20.32 mm pitch. Only parts under 3 mm tall (R7, the flat crystal Y1) are under it.
  - DS1307 RTC with a CR2032 in a Keystone 103 holder, on I2C1 pulled up to 3.3 V.
  - ACT and PWR LEDs, and a 1×4 spare header for GP22/GP28.
- **Checks:** ERC is clean.
- **Board:**
  - 160 × 100 mm Eurocard, with the Pico's USB port at the front edge.
  - Freerouting routed it in 28 s.
  - DRC with schematic parity gives 0 violations, 0 unconnected pads and 0 footprint errors. References D2 and D3 were hidden.
  - Views: `trs80_card_sd_routed.svg` and `trs80_card_sd_3d.png`.
- **Firmware** (`firmware/sdcard`, Pico SDK + FatFs R0.15): an independent implementation of the FreHD protocol. See its README for the supported commands.
  - Host tests: 79 checks pass, including read-only DMK/JV3/JV1 floppy images, and the Pico sources pass a syntax check (`test/build_test.ps1`, MSVC).
  - Builds with Pico SDK 2.3.1 and Arm GCC 15.3 (`firmware/sdcard/build.ps1`) into `frehd_pico.uf2`, about 64 KB, with no warnings at `-Wall -Wextra`. Not yet run on hardware.

**Check before ordering the SD card:**
1. The SD module's pin order against J2.
2. The Keystone 103 battery-holder footprint against the holder you buy.
3. Bus timing on the first board. SETTLE_CYCLES and DRIVE_CYCLES in `bus.c` give about 400–480 ns for the 2N7000 edges.

## Daughter card 3: 512 KB banked memory (X-MEM/80 compatible)

`hardware/card-xmem`, generated by `gen_card_xmem.py` and `gen_card_xmem_pcb.py`.

The card is software-compatible with X-MEM/80 for the Model I ([lambdamikel/x-mem-80](https://github.com/lambdamikel/x-mem-80), GPL-3.0). The port interface comes from that project's README. The circuit is my own design for ECB, not a copy of the original.

**Modes** (JP1 selects; set it to match the software):

| Mode | JP1 | Ports | Paging |
|---|---|---|---|
| X-MEM | open | OUT 41h → page at 8000h–BFFFh; OUT 42h → page at C000h–FFFFh | 32 pages of 16 KB |
| SuperMem | fitted | OUT 43h → page at 8000h–FFFFh; IN 43h reads it back | 16 pages of 32 KB |

- **Address map:** only 8000h–FFFFh is banked, so with the Model I's own 16 KB, ROM BASIC sees 48 KB.
- **Software:** MEMRES/CMD, Hyperdrive (SuperMem mode), Sidekick, LeScript and the MIDI/80 loaders should run as on the original.

**Circuit**
- **Memory:** U1 AS6C4008, 512K × 8, 55 ns. U2 74HCT245 buffers its data onto ECB.
- **Page registers:** U5 (8000h window) and U6 (C000h window) are 74HCT273s.
  - RESET* clears both, so the pages are defined at power-up. On the original they power up undefined.
  - U7, a 74HCT157, feeds SRAM A15–A18 from U5 or U6 according to CPU A14.
  - U8, a 74HCT244, drives the IN 43h readback. U3 gates it with ENABLE (JP2), so a disabled card never drives the data bus.
- **U3 GAL** (`ecb_xmem_io.pld`): decodes ports 41h–43h, qualified by IORQ* with M1* high. The register clocks fire on the leading edge of WR*, when the OUT data is already valid.
- **U4 GAL** (`ecb_xmem_mem.pld`):
  - SRAM CE = MREQ* & A15 & (RD* | WR*) & EN.
  - SRAM A14 is CPU A14 in SuperMem mode, or the selected register's bit 4 in X-MEM mode.
- **Page mapping:** X-MEM page n is 16 KB block {n3..n0, n4}, and SuperMem page p is 32 KB block p. Every page is distinct. SuperMem page bit 4 is ignored, so pages 00h/10h are the same physical page, as the original's notes describe.
- **Expansion Interface RAM:** on the Model I, X-MEM/80 hides the EI's own RAM by not passing RD*/WR* for 8000h–FFFFh to the EI.
  - On ECB, the card holds ECB c10 (EXTMEM*) low while it is enabled (JP2).
  - With JP8/JP9 on 2-3, the backplane bridge GAL (`ecb_bridge.pld` rev 2.1) then withholds memory RD*/WR* for 8000h–FFFFh from the EI on J2. A 4.7k pull-up (backplane R2) keeps c10 high when no such card is fitted.
  - c10 is a spare line in the RetroBrew pinout, so check no other ECB card you fit uses it.
- **Board:** 160 × 100 mm Eurocard. Freerouting routed it in 100 s. DRC with schematic parity gives 0 violations, 0 unconnected pads and 0 footprint errors. ERC is clean.
- **Check before ordering:**
  - The AS6C4008 DIP-32 pinout. I used the JEDEC 628512 layout and couldn't confirm it from a datasheet.
  - Bus timing. The original notes that Model I memory timing is tight. The card adds the backplane's 74HCT244 address buffers (about 20 ns) and the GAL (about 15 ns) to the path, so use 55 ns SRAM and -15 or faster GALs.

## Mechanics

Nothing in the design fixes card guides or subrack mounting: the 160 mm cards are held only by their DIN connectors. Use a 3U subrack with card guides, or add guide rails, before putting the system together. Slot pitch is 20.32 mm, and the tallest card parts (Tang Nano 20K and Pico on sockets, the flat SD module) are about 12–14 mm, which leaves about 3 mm to the next card.

## Daisy chains and the spare slots

Our cards don't use BUSRQ, BAI/BAO or IEI/IEO, so they just link chain-in to chain-out. Slots 3–5 are kept for future cards (sound, RS-232, a second SD card…).

## Decisions (agreed 2026-11-13)

1. **SD card controller: Raspberry Pi Pico** on headers. Its PIO handles the bus, and the firmware is C with a FAT library.
2. **Data bus: unbuffered pass-through** on the backplane. Each card buffers its own data.
3. **Card size: 100 × 160 mm Eurocards.**
4. **Slots: 5** (3 agreed at first, widened to 5 on 2026-11-13). Connector and pinout follow the reference ECB backplane, and all through-hole parts rules apply.

**Build order:** backplane, then the FPGA card (Tang Nano 20K), then the SD card (hardware and Pico firmware).

## What happens to rev 0.1

Rev 0.1 stays in the repository as the single-board design. Rev 2 becomes three KiCad projects (`hardware/backplane`, `hardware/card-fpga`, `hardware/card-sd`). The FPGA design is re-targeted to the GW2AR-18 (new pin constraints and device, same Verilog). The Z80 software doesn't change, apart from the SD drivers, which already exist for FreHD.
